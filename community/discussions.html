Consensus trade-offs: Raft vs. Paxos in high-throughput partitioned metadata quorums

When designing partitioned metadata storage engines capable of exceeding 500,000 IOPS under sustained write-skew, leader heartbeats and log-compaction replication introduce noticeable tail-latency spikes. Raft simplifies state synchronization through strict linear leader succession, but Multi-Paxos allows pipelined proposals when leader continuity remains stable.

We observed that during asymmetric WAN degradations where follower nodes encounter packet loss rates between 2% and 4%, Raft's election timer oscillations can cause cascading quorum renegotiations. What architectural strategies are peer teams utilizing to suppress false-positive leader depose cycles without compromising split-brain safety bounds?

In our benchmarks on TiKV-style Raft engines, we addressed the asymmetric partition hazard by introducing pre-vote extensions (Ongaro Section 9.6). Followers perform an uncommitted election probe before incrementing the actual term counter, effectively preventing transient WAN jitter from demoting an active cluster leader.
